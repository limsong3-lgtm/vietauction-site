About VietAuction

A catalogue that belongs to Vietnam

VietAuction organizes auctions of antique ceramics, with a focus on Vietnamese and Chinese pieces from the 7th to the 20th century. We work with owners and collectors across the country, and our first sale, Ceramic Legacies: Echoes of Time, was held at the Majestic Hotel in Ho Chi Minh City on 10 May 2026.

Every piece we handle is examined by an appraiser, measured, photographed and given a permanent VietAuction item number. Certificates from any appraisal organization are recorded against that number, so a piece keeps one identity from the day it reaches us. The same numbers power the public catalogue at vietauction.org.

Cultural heritage

A thousand years of Vietnamese clay

Ceramics are one of the longest unbroken records of Vietnamese life. The pieces in our auctions follow five stages of that story, and the figure beside each stage is the number of lots in Echoes of Time 2 that belong to it.

  1. 7th–10th century4 lots

    Early glazed wares

    Glazed stoneware from the first millennium shows how long potters in the Red River region have worked with fire and glaze, long before the first independent dynasties.

  2. 11th–14th century7 lots

    Lý and Trần celadon and white wares

    Under the Lý (1009–1225) and Trần (1225–1400) courts, potters refined celadon and ivory-white glazes for temples, palaces and daily life.

  3. 15th–16th century39 lots

    Blue-and-white and the trade routes

    Kilns such as Chu Đậu in Hải Dương made blue-and-white and painted wares that travelled by sea across Southeast Asia and beyond. A 15th-century cargo found off Cù Lao Chàm in the 1990s is the best-known evidence of that trade.

  4. 17th–19th century22 lots

    Nguyễn court and trade porcelain

    Ports such as Hội An brought Chinese export porcelain into Vietnamese homes, and the Nguyễn court at Huế (1802–1945) commissioned its own fine blue-and-white services.

  5. 20th century1 lot

    Modern collecting

    Late wares record how older forms continued in workshops and homes into the modern era.

Why it matters

What a healthy local market gives back

Antiques that have a trusted place to be bought and sold at home stay in Vietnam, stay documented and stay valued. This is the market we are building.

Heritage stays at home

When owners and collectors can sell openly in Vietnam, fine pieces have less reason to leave through informal channels. More of the national record remains in the country.

Trust through records

Permanent item numbers, certificates and a logged chain of owners make authentic pieces easier to prove and copies harder to pass off.

Fair value for families

Families who hold inherited pieces get a public, contract-backed sale instead of private offers, and buyers know what they are paying for.

Shared knowledge

Measurements, descriptions, photographs and provenance are kept in a searchable catalogue that students, museums and researchers can learn from.

Skilled local work

Appraisal, conservation, photography, publishing and events create skilled jobs and bring collectors, and tourism spending, to Vietnamese cities.
